# The Weekly Timetable

**The hour budget behind [THE_QUANT_BUILD_MANUAL.md](THE_QUANT_BUILD_MANUAL.md). Written 21 October 2026, for 26 November 2026 to 1 January 2028.**

Four pillars, in strict priority order. When they collide, the one higher up wins — always, without debating it in the moment.

| # | Pillar | Hours per week | Negotiable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sleep** | 56 (8 × 7) | **No.** Ever. |
| 2 | **Schoolwork** | 40 | No. Your degree is the asset. |
| 3 | **Build** (code, or reading Claude output) | 14–30 | Yes — this is the shock absorber |
| 4 | **Body and output** | 6.5 (5 exercise, 1.5 public writing) | No, but it is small |

---

## 1. Where the 168 hours go

Not an estimate. Arithmetic, with the fixed costs of being alive included: meals 10.5h, commute 5h, hygiene and chores 5.25h.

| Mode | Sleep | School | Build | Fixed | Body/output | **Genuinely free** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | 56 | 40 | 14 | 20.8 | 6.5 | **30.8h — 4.4h/day** |
| **Sprint** | 56 | 40 | 30 | 20.8 | 6.5 | **14.8h — 2.1h/day** |

That is the honest picture of the top of your range: **2 hours a day of actual freedom**. Everything else is accounted for. A sprint week is a 70-hour working week — 11.7 hours a day across six days.

### The trade you will be tempted by, priced

Cut sleep to 7 hours and you buy **1 extra hour a day**, taking free time from 2.1h to 3.1h. In exchange you lose the thing that makes the other 70 hours produce anything. You already know from this project that a tired reviewer is exactly who ships a 92%-accuracy leak and celebrates it.

**Sleep is pillar 1 for a reason. Do not trade it.** If the week will not fit, cut build hours.

---

## 2. Two modes, and the rule for switching

You cannot run 70-hour weeks for 57 straight weeks. Nobody can, and the people who claim they do are either lying or producing nothing in the last 20 hours. **The 14–30 range is not a target to max out — it is the mechanism that makes 14 months survivable.**

### Default cycle: three baseline weeks, one sprint

| Week in block | Mode | Build hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 | Baseline | 14 each |
| 4 | Sprint | 30 |

That is **72 build hours per 4-week block, averaging 18/week**. Over the full 57 weeks: roughly **1,000 hours of build time**, which is more than the plan needs.

### Forced sprints

Sprint weeks are not chosen by mood. Schedule one in the week **before each phase gate** in the manual — Gate 0, 1a, 1b, 2, 3, 4, 5. If a sprint lands next to a deadline or an exam, it moves. It never stacks.

## 3. The weekday template

Adapt the university block to your real lecture timetable — this assumes a 09:00–17:00 day at Mile End.

| Time | Baseline | Sprint |
|---|---|---|
| 07:00 | Wake | Wake |
| 07:00–07:45 | Breakfast, out the door | Same |
| 07:45–08:45 | Commute — **Block A reading**, no keyboard | Same |
| 09:00–17:00 | **University: lectures, tutorials, library (8h)** | Same |
| 17:00–18:00 | Commute home, decompress | Same |
| 18:00–19:00 | Dinner. Away from the desk. | Same |
| 19:00–21:00 | **Build — 2h** | **Build — 3h** (to 22:00) |
| 21:00–23:00 | Free | 22:00–23:00 wind down |
| 23:00–07:00 | **Sleep — 8h** | **Sleep — 8h** |

Five weekdays × 8h = **the full 40 schoolwork hours**. Weekends carry no schoolwork in either mode, which is what makes the sprint survivable.

### The commute is a real asset

Block A of the three-block protocol — theory, derivation, no keyboard — fits the train exactly. Forty minutes each way, five days, is **over 6 hours a week** of reading you would otherwise never find. That is where López de Prado chapter 7 and the Gu, Kelly and Xiu paper get read.

## 4. The weekend

| | Baseline | Sprint |
|---|---|---|
| **Saturday** | 09:00–13:00 build (4h), then exercise and your own life | 09:00–13:00 and 14:00–18:00 build (**8h**) |
| **Sunday** | **Off entirely.** 30 min review in the evening. | 10:00–17:00 build (**7h**), 30 min review |

**Baseline Sundays are completely off.** That is deliberate and it is not a reward you earn — it is what keeps the other 51 weeks possible. Protect it as hard as you protect sleep.

### Sunday review, 30 minutes, written into `routine/progress_log.md`

1. Which gate am I working toward, and what is the number right now?
2. What did I measure this week that I did not know last week?
3. What did I try that failed? *(Nothing failed means nothing was tested.)*
4. Cumulative model configurations tried, total?
5. Did I drift into something fun and measurable instead of the actual gate?

Question 5 catches the commonest failure. Polishing the simulator feels productive and is measurable, which is exactly why six weeks can disappear into it while the gate goes untouched.

## 5. The fourth pillar

**Exercise — 5 hours, three sessions.** Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday. This is not self-care decoration; at 70-hour weeks it is the thing that determines whether week 30 still produces work. It is the first thing people cut and the cut that costs most.

**Public output — 1.5 hours, one piece.** One short technical write-up a week. Not motivational posts: one measurement, one finding, one thing you got wrong. You already have a year's worth of material — the leakage demonstration alone is a better post than 99% of what students publish.

Over 57 weeks that is **a 57-post record of real quantitative work**, which is the single highest-return use of 1.5 hours a week you have available.

## 6. Exam override — May and June 2027

During revision and exams, the build drops to **zero to 5 hours a week** and schoolwork absorbs everything. This is not a concession, it is in the plan: the manual's Phase 3 marks weeks 25–26 as reduced load with reading only.

A missed exam costs more than a delayed phase. There is no version of this where the project is worth a grade.

---

## 7. When it breaks — the order of sacrifice, decided now

You will fall behind. Deciding this in advance means not having to decide it at 23:00 on a Tuesday while behind and tired.

1. **Never cut sleep.** Priced in section 1. It is a losing trade.
2. **Never cut schoolwork.** The degree is the asset the project decorates.
3. **Cut build hours first.** Drop a sprint to baseline, or baseline to zero for one week.
4. **Cut build scope before build quality.** Fewer features on a clean panel beats more features on a broken one — manual, Part IV.3.
5. **Keep exercise and the weekly review even in a bad week.** They are 5.5 hours and they are what pull you out.

### Two weeks off, scheduled

Christmas 2026 and one week in August 2027. **Zero build hours, by plan, not by collapse.** Put them in the calendar now or they will not happen.

---

## 8. The thing more hours cannot buy

A warning specific to this project, and it matters more than the schedule.

**Some of the plan is effort-limited. Some is not.**

Building the panel, constructing features, writing the validation harness — those are effort-limited. More hours genuinely means faster progress, and the sprint weeks pay off.

But the detection floor is not effort-limited. You measured it on 20 October: finding a signal the size real markets carry needs 20,000 to 80,000 training bars, against the 1,250 that five years of daily data gives you. **No number of hours moves that.** Thirty-hour build weeks will not make daily direction predictable, because the limit is sample size relative to signal, not your effort.

So spend the sprint weeks on the things hours can actually move — the panel, the features, the validation — and never on grinding at a wall the arithmetic already says is solid.

## 9. The weekly scorecard

Tick these on Sunday. Four of five is a good week.

- [ ] Slept 7.5h+ on at least six of seven nights
- [ ] Hit the schoolwork block all five weekdays
- [ ] Build hours within the mode's target (14 baseline / 30 sprint)
- [ ] Three exercise sessions
- [ ] Review written, including something that failed

**Two consecutive weeks under three out of five means the schedule is wrong, not you.** Drop to baseline, take a full Sunday, and re-read section 7.
